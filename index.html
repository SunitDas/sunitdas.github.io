Research Directions

My work is organized around three questions, each asking how the geometry of Bloch wavefunctions shows up in something measurable. Full descriptions are on the Research page.

Direction 1

Higher-order quantum geometry, nonlinear transport and optics

Beyond the Berry curvature and the quantum metric lies a family of higher-order and field-dressed geometric quantities: the Berry connection polarizability, layer- and orbital-resolved geometry, and their field-induced corrections. These control nonlinear conductivity, second-harmonic generation, and photocurrent responses. I work out which geometric object sources which response, under what symmetry conditions it survives, and how an experiment can isolate it.

Direction 2

Correlated quantum geometry and superconducting transport

Quantum geometry does not disappear when interactions become strong; it reorganizes. In flat-band and moiré systems, geometric quantities feed directly into superfluid stiffness, Josephson coupling, and nonreciprocal superconducting transport. I am interested in how correlations reshape the geometric contribution, and in what diode-like and nonlinear superconducting responses reveal about the underlying wavefunctions.

Direction 3

Spin, orbital, and altermagnetic responses

Charge is only one of several currents a crystal can carry. Spin and orbital angular momentum transport, light-induced magnetization, and current-driven switching of magnetic order all have quantum-geometric origins. Altermagnets are a particularly rich setting, combining nonrelativistic spin splitting with vanishing net moment, and I study the transport signatures and switching mechanisms they permit.
